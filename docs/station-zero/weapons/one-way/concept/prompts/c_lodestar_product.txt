Use case: product-mockup
Asset type: concept design image for a video game weapon, shown to the studio lead to choose a direction
Primary request: a clean three-quarter product photograph of one hand-held device. The object is a transit ticket-issuing machine rebuilt into a hand-held weapon. It must clearly show ALL of these working parts: (1) a pistol grip with a trigger; (2) an issuing lever, a sprung metal lever that snaps down to stamp and eject a ticket; (3) a ticket slot at the front where a gun's muzzle would be, a flat horizontal slot with the edge of one buff-coloured card ticket sticking out; (4) a ticket stack: a spring-loaded column of small stiff buff card tickets (each about 57 x 30 mm) seen through a window, with a follower plate; (5) a hinged stock door over that column; (6) a guard's dome bell, about 60 mm across, with an external striker hammer; (7) a row of exactly six small round amber indicator lamps; (8) a carrying strap. No gun barrel, no cartridge magazine, no optics.
Subject: Direction C, "the Lodestar terminal": a chunky short bullpup body like a piece of 1984 computer and telephone equipment: injection-moulded warm beige and putty-grey ABS plastic with ribbed vents, chocolate-brown rocker switches, a coiled telephone-style cable from the grip to the body, and a smoked brown acrylic stock door over the ticket column on top. The issuing lever is a wide brown paddle lever on top, like a card-reader handle. The bell is a classic telephone ringer: one polished steel dome gong with its striker, mounted exposed on the left side. The six amber lamps are a row of square pilot lights with a brushed aluminium bezel. A grey webbing shoulder strap. A small silver badge shows an eight-point compass star and the word "LODESTAR".
Style/medium: photorealistic studio product photograph, as in a 1986 industrial design annual. It is designed and built as a real manufactured product by a mid-1980s industrial designer working for a city transit authority: honest mechanical construction, visible fasteners, parting lines, engraved or screen-printed legends, wear from years of daily use. Believable and buildable. Era 1986.
Scene/backdrop: seamless neutral light grey studio sweep, nothing else in frame
Composition/framing: 16:9 landscape; the whole object in frame with a margin, front three-quarter view from the left-front and slightly above, ticket slot pointing to the left of frame, resting on the grey surface with a soft contact shadow
Lighting/mood: large softbox key from upper left, gentle fill, crisp speculars on the metal, neutral and factual
Color palette: beige and putty ABS plastic, chocolate brown, smoked acrylic, brushed aluminium, polished steel
Materials/textures: real material response, fine surface texture, edge wear, fingerprints on polished parts, slight grime in recesses
Constraints: exactly one object; every part listed must be visible and readable; exactly six amber lamps; one dome bell; the card tickets are plain buff card
Avoid: fantasy ornament, runes, skulls, glowing energy, sci-fi panels, neon, holograms, steampunk gears and pipes, any real brand name or logo, modern tactical rails, text other than what is specified, watermark.
