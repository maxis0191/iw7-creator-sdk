## The Zero Kings

No longer a vest-and-mohawk street gang. They are **HRT's own livery painters**, the paint shop crew sacked in
the 1979 cuts, who never left: they squatted the dead-end siding and went on painting, first the authority's
colours, then their own. Outfit M3: paint-crusted HRT paint-shop overalls, respirator pushed up, a crown
stencilled on the back. They stopped painting in December 1984. The discovery that the dead obey colour was
made by one surviving King, "Undercoat", during 1985, who painted warnings (the murals) and built the paint rig;
whether Undercoat is alive is not stated.

## Lore corrections

- The express bypass was driven **around** Vayle's tube, wound beside it like a pipe beside an organ pipe; the
  Carrier leaked into the tuned tube. A six-car train never ran inside the eleven-seat tube. The Capsule Post
  uses the tube's small dispatch bores.
- The Parlour's signature objects are no longer a grand piano and a goldfish fountain. They are **the fork
  wall** (144 steel tuning forks in a walnut frame, each stamped with a passenger class and a pitch; Vayle had
  every passenger "tuned" before boarding) and **the listening horn** (a brass horn the size of a carriage
  door, facing a ring of velvet seats). One fork rings by itself when a zombie passes.
- **The Terminus** is no longer a frozen gala. It is the station **as a timetable knows it**: a white, shadowless
  platform with nothing on it that a timetable does not contain: the platform edge, the yellow line, perfect
  signs ("TERMINUS", "WAY OUT" pointing at a wall), numbered benches, a clock, and on the floor 312 pairs of
  painted footprints in rows, each numbered. Four more pairs are freshly painted in the heroes' colours when
  the players first arrive (eight pairs with eight players). No banquet, no band, no guest book, no waltz. Its
  clock runs from 23:59:20 and **stops at 23:59:59**: the visit (40 s) ends one second before midnight. The run
  has still never arrived (hook H3 stays open).
- Winding the clocks makes ZERO **expect** the arrival and open boarding; it does not log one. The board reads
  "047 · DUE 00:00 · NOW --:--" while the clocks are wound and "047 · TERMINUS · BOARDING" once the Handle is
  fitted, never "ARRIVAL".
- The Dead-Man's Handle: ZERO needed no driver, so the handle was ceremonial: the conductor carried it on a
  cushion to hand to the Chairman at midnight. That is why the Conductor has it, and why he walks to the cab.
- The other platforms of the station (it had four lines) are behind the Cordon's concrete plugs. The night
  staff on the concourse died when the Carrier's chime played to them at 23:47: it reaches everyone in earshot
  of the public address. The Zero Kings in the siding were spared; the few who were up on the concourse are the M3 dead, outside the 510.
- "Each beside something he built" (Vayle's pages) becomes "where his work was found".
- Pay phones: three, not six (Fare Hall, Police Post, Car Barn), each with a different event.
- Collections: 8 tapes, 6 pages, 5 murals, 3 other documents, 3 trophies = **25**.

## The Night Inspector (redesign)

Not a tall figure in a long coat, not a stalker through doors. **Short, very wide, lamp-headed**: a stout
man-shape in a brass-buttoned HRT tunic that no longer closes, short legs, long arms, and where the head
should be a **hand signal lamp** with a peaked cap sitting on top of it. Right arm: the hydraulic ticket punch.
A satchel trails a paper ribbon of punched tickets behind him. He **hurries**: a brisk waddle at 150 units per
second and, every 10 s, a 2 s scuttle at 260. He arrives and moves between zones by climbing out of brass
**inspection plates** in the floor (one per zone), never through doors. Mark: the player who **earned** the
most points in the last two rounds (not unspent points); players who are away, downed or in the Terminus are
skipped. Fines go to a pile only the fined player can collect when he dies. Health is flat and capped.

## The Capsule Post (redesign)

The rider **chooses the destination** on a brass address dial at the hatch (any unsealed hatch). The tubes are
a ring: a destination one segment away along the Bellows' current direction takes 5 s and 250 points, two
segments 9 s and 400, three segments 13 s and 550. The BLOW / DRAW lever reverses which destinations are near;
nothing is ever unreachable, so the lever cannot be used to trap the team. No slow-down on arrival (weapon
raise 1 s only). Blowback takes only zombies within 48 units of the hatch.

## Balance rules

- Wonder-weapon damage is **flat**: each effect is rated at a round R and deals the full health of an ordinary
  round-R zombie (stock formula); upgraded forms raise R. No effect kills "at any round". Damage to the
  Inspector is flat too.
- EXACT CHANGE: its hits pay no hit points; pierced kills pay half; refund on the first target's head-shot kill
  only; dropped tokens (upgraded) at most one per second per player.
- LIVE RAIL: one hit per zombie per fence per 2 s; a stunned zombie is pushed off the line; fences trip
  Rollers and do not kill them; only AMBER affects specials.
- TOP TO BOTTOM: YELLOW bursts do not tag; one burst generation only; CYAN trips Rollers, does not kill.
- The Terminus visit is 40 s with a 150 s cooldown; downed players travel with the team; zombies in car 1 are
  pushed out, not killed; the round's spawning pauses while every player is away.
- Rollers: when every living player stays out of skating reach for 10 s, Rollers throw (a spare skate, 30
  damage, from outside). Lost Property never returns a wonder weapon. Sour Grudges ignores wonder-weapon kills.
- Named test cases for the degenerate combinations are in the plans of WW1 to WW4 and P1.

## Second round (after the final verification pass)

- **The Atmospheric is a pneumatic parcel post**, not a passenger railway in a tube: a ring of dispatch tubes
  with a trunk main under the plaza; for one season (1897) it sold "passenger dispatch" in padded capsules.
  The disaster is "the Silent Dispatch": eleven capsules came home with their riders dead and smiling. There
  is no round wooden car and no eleven seats: there is **capsule No. 12**, Vayle's own, on a cradle in the
  Parlour's arch, the only one with a window on the inside (the old "seat 12" secret and hook H2).
- **The Terminus is made of printout**: every surface is green-bar fan-fold printer paper at room size,
  everything on it printed in dot-matrix, every time 23:59; flat office light; footprints printed, the
  players' in wet ink; the Pack-a-Punch the only solid coloured object. Not a white station. Its clock runs
  23:59:19 to 23:59:59 (40 s). Its sound: a line printer that never stops, the low Departure Note, the Voice.
- Names: Orbit Rollerdrome becomes **Orbit Rink**; the painter Primer becomes **Undercoat**; the perk Day
  Trippers becomes **BON VOYAGES**; the Dispatcher's upgrade becomes **FORWARDING ADDRESS**; the emote Roller
  Boogie becomes **Rink Glide**.
- The murals were painted by Undercoat in 1985 (all five); the tubes are drawn from memory.
- The train register in the Signal Cabin is open at 23:44. The 23:47 carried 312 people: 308 guests, crew of 4.
- THE DISPATCHER: a send uses up its stamps and has a 3 s cooldown. EXACT CHANGE: the refund is the base 30,
  once per 1.5 s, only for a one-token head kill. YELLOW bursts hit the six nearest. Wonder-weapon hits on the
  Night Inspector are capped at 2,000 with no lamp bonus.
- Capsule Post: 45 s rest per rider between rides; Bon Voyages gives half-price postage, not free; a marked
  player keeps the Inspector's mark through a ride or a Terminus visit. Decon Foam: zombies attack at half rate.
- Rollers circle outside and throw as they pass, at each player who is out of skating reach.
- The Inspector follows a marked player to another zone 6 s after they arrive there (one number everywhere).
