# Orbit Rink (and the Rollers)

**Orbit Rink** was a roller rink on Marlow Street, Port Halcyon, opened in 1979 by Gus Brandvold, a former dance-hall manager, and kept open through the fiscal crisis by sheer volume of birthday parties. Its motto, painted over the door beneath a ringed planet, was **"Roll Into Tomorrow"**. By 1984 roller discos were in their last year of fashion and Brandvold knew it.

## The charter

In November 1984 Brandvold bought a charter of the sixth car of the inaugural Zero Line train for the Rink's New Year **"Roll-In"**, as publicity, at a price the Rapid, which needed the money, did not negotiate. He drew the guests from his regulars: forty skaters in satin bomber jackets in Orbit purple with a ringed-planet back patch, headbands, tube socks, white quad skates with amber wheels and glow necklaces. Instead of coats they were asked to bring their skates. They boarded car 6, the **Party Car**, at 23:42 and left their street shoes in a neat row inside the door.

The car had streamers, a mirror ball and a sound system, and the playlist for the first thirteen minutes had been timed to the second by Brandvold's daughter, so that the last track would end on the stroke of midnight.

## The Rollers

After the Midnight Service, and for as long as ZERO is between departures, the party goes on. The forty skaters are the dead that staff and cleaners came to call **the Rollers**. The Club Kids who came to the party without skates stand on foot among the ordinary dead. They cannot stop and they cannot turn sharply. They come in lines, hands on shoulders, a leader whooping like a referee, and the wheels glow amber for the same reason all the eyes do. A Roller is fast on a straight surface and helpless against a wall. A Roller does not take stairs, because nobody ever skated stairs at the Orbit. When every player is out of reach of the wheels, the Rollers circle outside and throw a spare skate through the opening as they pass, at each player who is out of reach.

Cleanup logs describe the sound as the thing that gave them away: polyurethane wheels on tile, heard a hundred metres off, before the first line came round a column.

## The Roll-In record

The Orbit's sign-in sheet, signed at the rink before the party left for the station, is still in the tray of its booth on Marlow Street. It has forty signatures, a drawing of a ringed planet and the line "back by twelve!" in a child's hand.

## In the map

The Roller round is the special round of the map: the first one in about round 6, then every five to seven rounds. They roll out of car 6 of the 23:47 and out of walk-in spawners in each active zone, in conga chains, and the round ends with a Max Ammo drop and 500 points per player. The Rollers are fast on Platform A and in the Car Barn. Three brass skate-wheel trophies from the Orbit party are hidden in three zones: Platform A East, the Car Barn and the Sump.
