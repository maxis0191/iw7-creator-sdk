# The Carrier (and the Three-Tone)

The **Carrier** is the control signal of the Zero Line: a low audio tone injected by [ZERO](zero.md) into the running rails and the public address lines of the station, so that the machine can find every train and every lamp on the network by listening for the answer. It is a fault-finding tool, and a good one. Lodestar engineers called it "the line's pulse".

## What it is, physically

The Carrier is a **lamp-proving current**: a small steady current that lets ZERO check that every signal lamp, door lamp and headsign lamp is burning. Where a lamp burns, the Carrier is visible in it. The eyes of the dead glow signal amber for the same reason, and for no reason that the Rapid's engineers have been willing to write down.

## What it does to the dead

The Carrier moves the dead. While it plays and the boards say "boarding", they walk to where their timetable puts them. Anything between them and the platform edge is an obstruction to be cleared. When ZERO is between departures the Carrier drops to a hum and they stand down. That is why the nights of Station Zero are divided into rounds.

## The meeting

In October 1983 [Dr. Imogen Strand](dr-imogen-strand.md) fed the Carrier into the 1893 tube and recorded that it came back changed. The tube had been tuned by [Ambrose Vayle](ambrose-vayle.md) to a single low C, the **Departure Note**, which is felt in the chest and not heard. The Carrier and the Note do not interfere; they agree. The tube was never driven through, only passed beside, and the Carrier leaks into it through the wall; a train carrying the Carrier down the bypass beside it arrives somewhere first. Her tapes describe the result as "an answer", and the Midnight Service is its only demonstration.

## The Three-Tone

The **Three-Tone** is the Carrier made audible: three falling notes sounded on every public address horn before each announcement. Lodestar's sound department designed it in 1984 as a friendly chime and tested it on forty volunteers, who rated it "reassuring". Its three notes do not make a chord, and sit a shade out of true; the tester noted it and left it. It is also the sound that starts a departure. Staff who have heard it in the empty station report that the building seems to turn to listen.

## Reading the Carrier

A rule of thumb from the Lodestar cleanup logs: if the horns are chiming, move; if the board says "boarding", get out of the way. A dead commuter on the Carrier is not angry. He is simply certain that you are on his platform.

## In the map

The Three-Tone is the round-start sound; the pause between rounds is ZERO between departures. Winding a clock plays it and brings a burst of zombies in that zone. The Bellows organ sounds the Departure Note under every Capsule Post launch and when the lever changes its direction. The amber eyes of the dead are the Carrier's lamps.
