# 00 · STATION ZERO: THE PITCH

**A Willard Wyler picture, 1986. "Next stop: nowhere."**

## Premise

New Year's Eve, 1985, Port Halcyon. Under a walled-off plaza lies Station 0 of the city's metro, sealed a year
ago after a "gas incident". The truth: the first driverless train in the country, the 23:47, was sent round
a bypass wound beside a forgotten Victorian pneumatic tube, that its builder had tuned to send passengers where the dead go. It
came back in the same second with 312 people who were dead, standing and still listening to the station
announcer. Every clock stopped at 23:47. The dispatch computer, ZERO, cannot log a failed run, so it
keeps announcing the departure, and each announcement gets the dead to their feet. Four people go down
tonight for four bad reasons: a transit cop, a graffiti king, a signal engineer and a TV reporter.

## Tone

A straight-faced 1980s creature feature with one joke under it: the dead are commuters. They are not hungry.
They are late. Rounds are departures; the round chime is the station's own three-tone; between rounds the
dead wait behind the yellow line for a train that is already there.

## What a player does

**In the first 5 minutes.** Wakes on a rain-soaked sunken plaza under a quarantine canopy, next to a news
van with its door open. Buys a pistol and the revive sweet, goes down the lit entrance of "STATION 0" into a
dark ticket hall where the only light is a six-metre split-flap board that talks to them: "DEPARTURE 1 ·
POWER: G-2 OFFLINE · CONVERTER HALL →". Follows real station signs down dead escalators and sees it: a
silver train, clean, lit from inside, doors open. Fights along the platform to a hall where a diesel set
wears a "DO NOT RESTART" tag, restarts it, and watches the station come on zone by zone, two glass rectifiers
striking violet.

**At round 10.** The team has wound four stopped clocks forward the missing thirteen minutes, set the road
in the signal cabin, killed the Conductor for his dead-man's handle and boarded the 23:47, which does not
move and takes them anyway: to the Terminus, the station as a timetable knows it: a platform made of green-bar printer paper, every
column a column of times, a clock that stops one second before midnight, their own footprints already
printed on the floor, and the Pack-a-Punch standing where a ticket validator should be. Back on the platform a brass plate
in the floor rattles and lifts. The Night Inspector climbs out: short, wide, a signal lamp where his head
should be with a cap sitting on it, a ribbon of punched tickets trailing behind, and he wants to see the
ticket of whoever has been earning the most.

**At round 25.** Seventy-eight dead commuters are in the fight. The team posts itself across the map in
one-person brass capsules, dialling an address at each hatch; plants arcs of standing third rail across
doorways; stamps an address on four of the dead and sends them flying to it; sprays the dead magenta so that
they go and wait behind the yellow line; and pays for every shot of a token gun with its own score. Every few
rounds the lights go to mirror-ball and the Orbit Rink's New Year party comes out of car 6 on skates,
in conga lines, unable to stop.

## What makes it its own

1. **One place, five eras, no repeats.** Thirteen distinct places in eighteen spawn zones: a street cordon, a 1978 shopping underpass, a
   police post, a 1961 fallout shelter, the platform and its train, a depot, a power hall, a computer
   exchange, a storm sump, the sacked painters' siding, an 1893 gilded salon and its blower vault. One platform.
   One train. It never moves.
2. **The station is the interface.** Objectives are on the departure board, wayfinding is the station's own
   signage, the visit to Pack-a-Punch is timed by the only running clock on the map.
3. **Fast travel that is post, not transit.** You dial an address and are sent; near is cheap and quick, far
   is dear and slow, and one lever swaps near for far for everyone; a zombie touching the hatch comes with you.
4. **A mini boss that reads the scoreboard**, and a special round that moves like nothing else in the game.
5. **Four wonder weapons with four verbs**: pay, place, send, paint. Four new perks built on things only this
   map has: the thirteenth hit, the Post and the traps, a grudge, a photograph.
6. **Hooks, not holes.** The main quest, the boss and the guest character are for later, and the map
   already says where they will come from: two clocks still unwound, a second platform in the fog, a lit
   "ON AIR" sign over a locked booth.

## Scale

18 spawn zones and the Terminus; about 19,500 m2 of gross plan area, of which about 15,000 m2 walkable (a little
over twice the present map; the generator prints the real figure); first version at the
shipped map's median prop density (6,000 props, 520 lights), full dressing 8,900; 17 doors; 10 perks; 21
wall-weapon spots; 4 traps; a target of 78 zombies. Numbers and their sources: `02-WORLD.md`.

## What a player on today's official game client gets (said plainly)

- **The map**: not yet by itself. Receiving a custom map from a public server is unproven on the official
  client; until that is proven or fixed in the client, the recommendation is private test servers only.
- **Seventy-eight zombies**: the target. On the official client it can only be the "hybrid crowd" (78 bodies
  of which at most 24, and 18 once specials are about, are engine zombies at a moment), and only if its first probes pass; otherwise the cap stays 24
  until the client itself can hold more. Real 78 needs a client change.
- **The full Tab screen and the emote wheel**: a server-drawn version (text pages and a simple picker). The
  designed screens need a client that loads Lua menus from a download.
- **Emotes**: none until four unproven steps pass (our animations playing in the game, on a real player, in
  third person).
- **Everything else** (quest, Capsule Post, perks, weapons, traps, stand-in special enemies): complete on the
  official client once the map is on it.

Details and the client work packages: `14-CLIENT-CAPABILITIES.md`.
