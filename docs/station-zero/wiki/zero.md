# ZERO

**ZERO**, the **Zone Electronic Routing Operator**, is the dispatch computer of the Zero Line, built by [Lodestar Signal & Telephone](lodestar-signal-and-telephone.md) under a contract won in March 1982 and run in shadow from 1 November 1984. It sets the points, times the doors, issues the departures and watches every train through the [Carrier](the-carrier.md).

## How it speaks

ZERO has no screen for the public and no voice of its own. It speaks only through the departure board in the Fare Hall, through lamps, through printers and through the recorded [Voice of the Rapid](the-voice-of-the-rapid.md). A lit lamp is a statement; a printed line is a record. The split-flap board is its face.

## What it is made of

Its core is a telephone exchange converted into a computer: racks of relays that click in travelling waves as they work, two tape drives, green phosphor terminals and a line printer. Lodestar's engineers liked the relays because a fault could be found by listening. The printer prints the timetable of the dead on fan-fold paper, and the paper, uncollected for a year, has filled the printer room to the knee.

One rack of the core has an empty bay. Lodestar's last shift was sent down in June 1985 to recover "the core". What the bay once held has not been recorded.

## The one thing it cannot do

The log specification allows a run two states, PENDING and ARRIVED. Strand read the specification in December 1984 and found no third state. ZERO cannot record a run as failed. Run 047 has not arrived, so it is pending, so ZERO issues the departure again, announces the boarding again, and waits to log the midnight arrival. It has done this since 23:47 on 31 December 1984 and will do it as long as it has power.

It has never logged an arrival. What it does when it logs one is not known.

There is a second thing it can be made to do, and it is smaller. When the four working clocks of the station are wound forward to midnight, ZERO takes the hint: it *expects* 047, lights the boarding lamps and opens boarding. It does not log an arrival. Expecting is a state it already had; an arrival is one it has never entered. The board reads "047 · DUE 00:00 · BOARDING" and nothing else.

## Behaviour

ZERO is polite, regular and unhurried. It does not appear to know the passengers are dead; it knows they are not on the train. Its printed manifest sometimes carries names that were never on the invitation list.

## In the map

ZERO lives in the Lodestar Exchange: six aisles of clicking relays, two turning tape drives, and a back room where the printer prints the current players' names on the passenger manifest. Its face is the split-flap board in the Fare Hall, which shows the round as a departure and states the next objective. Winding the four quest clocks makes it open boarding for 047, and it never records an arrival. One rack has an empty bay.
