# 01 · LORE of STATION ZERO

This is the canon. The wiki (`wiki/`) retells it in the in-world voice; if the two ever differ, this file wins.
Every name here is ours. Nothing is taken from another game or film; the only inherited element is the frame of
the base game itself (Willard Wyler's cursed films and his four actors), because this is a map for that game.

## 1. The frame

STATION ZERO is one more film by Willard Wyler: a 1986 "transit horror" B-movie that nobody remembers buying a
ticket for. The four actors wake up inside it, re-cast as a Transit Cop, a Graffiti King, a Signal Engineer and
a Night Desk Reporter. The film's tagline, on the poster in the spawn area: **"Next stop: nowhere."**

Tone: straight-faced 1980s creature feature with a satirical core. The dead here are commuters. They are not
hungry, they are *late*, and a machine keeps telling them their train is boarding.
Reason: gives every zombie, sound and objective one shared joke that is also the horror.

## 2. The one-paragraph truth

On New Year's Eve 1984 the city of Port Halcyon inaugurated the Zero Line, the first metro line in the country
run with no drivers, by a dispatch computer called ZERO To save money, its express bypass was driven close around a
forgotten 1893 pneumatic dispatch main found under the station, wound beside it like one pipe beside another: a
tube its Victorian builder had tuned like an organ pipe to "send passengers where the dead go, and bring them back". At 23:47 on 31 December 1984 the
inaugural train, run 047, left Platform A carrying 312 people on a thirteen-minute loop timed to bring them back
on the stroke of midnight. It entered the bypass. ZERO's control signal (the Carrier) leaked into the tuned tube beside it, and
the tube answered. The train reached a place that is not on the map (the Terminus) and was back at its platform in
the same second. Every clock in the station stopped at 23:47:00: by the clocks it never left, and midnight never
came. Its passengers were dead, standing, and still listening to the public address. ZERO cannot record a
run as failed and is still waiting to log the midnight arrival, so it keeps re-issuing the departure. The city
called it a gas main rupture, poured concrete into the entrances and put a quarantine canopy over the plaza.
One year later, on the night the film takes place, the station is still announcing trains.

Kept from the earlier map session because its signage was the best-reviewed part of the old map (6 to 7 of 10):
the time 23:47 on every clock, board and headsign, run number 047, the brand "ZERO LINE", generator G-2 with its
"DO NOT RESTART" tag, the night roster with an empty OUT column, the missing-person posters (Malone, Prescott,
Hughes: three of the 58 night staff), and the painters' name under the crown stencil, "ZERO KINGS".

## 4. The place, layer by layer (why each zone exists)

The map is a vertical cut through ninety years of one city block. Each layer was built by people who did not
know, or pretended not to know, what was under them.

1. **Street, 1985: the Cordon.** The plaza above the station, walled by concrete barriers and roofed by a
   quarantine canopy. Lodestar's cleanup crews worked from here until they stopped coming back up.
2. **Concourse, 1931 rebuilt 1978: the Fare Hall and the Underpass Arcade.** Token booths, turnstiles, the big
   split-flap departure board, and a shopping underpass (record shop, copy shop, doughnut counter, photo
   booth, shoe repair, lost property office). Dressed for the inauguration: bunting, a ribbon, a banquet table.
3. **Platforms, 1931: Platform A with the 23:47, the track bed, the Signal Cabin, the Transit Police post.**
4. **Works, 1931 to 1984: the Car Barn (depot), the Converter Hall (traction power), the Lodestar Exchange
   (relay and computer rooms where ZERO lives), Shelter 7 (a 1961 civil-defence fallout shelter the
   authority forgot it had), the Sump (pump room and storm drain).**
5. **Underneath, 1893: the Parlour and the Bellows Vault**, Vayle's gilded waiting salon and blower chamber,
   broken into by Lodestar's excavation in 1983 and wired into the Zero Line.
6. **Nowhere: the Terminus.** Reached only from the cab of the 23:47.

The station once served four lines; the other platforms lie behind the Cordon's concrete plugs. The night staff
on the concourse died at 23:47 with the passengers: the Carrier's chime reaches everyone in earshot of the
public address. The Zero Kings who were out of earshot in the siding were spared; the few who had gone up to the concourse
were not (the M3 dead), and are outside the count of 510.

## 5. The era, concretely

Late 1984 and 1985: sodium light, cigarette machines, pay phones, token economy, cassette decks, CRT monitors
with green phosphor, relay logic beside the first microprocessors, roller discos in their last year of fashion,
spray-painted trains, a city that cannot pay its police, and a transit authority selling automation as salvation.
Cold War leftovers (Shelter 7) sit one door away from a Victorian salon. Fonts and signs: a clean transit
grotesque for HRT, a compass-star corporate serif for Lodestar, hand-painted gilt for the Atmospheric.

## 6. Factions and people

- **Halcyon Rapid Transit.** Broke, proud, desperate for a success. Chairman Dellinger promised "a railway that
  is never late". He got one.
- **Lodestar Signal & Telephone.** A telephone-exchange maker moving into computers. Knew from Dr. Strand's
  1983 memo that the old tunnel "answers" the Carrier. Kept the bypass anyway: it saved eleven months.
  After the incident Lodestar ran the cleanup, which was really a recovery of its own equipment.
- **Dr. Imogen Strand.** Read Vayle's notebooks, understood them, warned her employer, then rode the 23:47
  herself in the cab "to keep a hand on it". Her tape diaries are the map's main lore pickups. Whether she is
  among the dead is deliberately not stated (hook).
- **Ambrose Vayle (1851 to 1897).** Built the Atmospheric as a parcel post and a private experiment. In 1897,
  on the last day of passenger dispatch, eleven capsules came home with their riders smiling and dead; Vayle's
  own capsule, No. 12, came home empty. The
  company folded and the tunnel was bricked up. His portrait hangs in the Parlour; its eyes have been cut out
  (by whom: hook).
- **The Voice of the Rapid (Evelyn Marsh).** A warm, exact, slightly amused recorded voice. ZERO assembles
  new sentences from her recordings. Sometimes a sentence was never recorded.
- **The Zero Kings.** The Rapid's own paint-shop crew, sacked in the 1979 cuts, who never left: they squatted
  the dead-end siding (the Boneyard) and went on painting, first the authority's colours, then their own. They
  stopped in December 1984. One of them, "Undercoat", lived through 1985 down there, learned that the dead obey
  the railway's colour code, and painted the murals as warnings. Whether Undercoat is alive is not stated.
- **Orbit Rink.** Its owner bought the Party Car charter as publicity. Forty skaters boarded in costume.
- **The Night Inspector.** Chief Fare Inspector **Horace Pell**, thirty-one years of service, who was to
  check the first ticket of the automated era. ZERO still issues him a duty roster.

## 8. What the zombies were

312 people on the 23:47 (308 guests and a crew of 4), 58 night staff of the station, and about 140 Lodestar cleanup workers sent in
over the following year. They are not infected and nothing spreads by bite. The Carrier moves them: while the
Three-Tone plays and the boards say "boarding", they walk to where their timetable puts them, and anything
between them and the platform edge is an obstruction to be cleared. Their eyes glow signal amber because the
Carrier is, physically, a lamp-proving current. When ZERO is between departures (the pause between rounds)
they stand down, which is why rounds exist.

## 9. Objects and machines (each has a wiki page)

- **The split-flap board** (Fare Hall): ZERO's face. Shows the round as a departure ("DEPARTURE 12 · ZERO
  LINE · BOARDING"). It is how the machine talks to the players.
- **The station clocks**: seven. Six stopped at 23:47:00 (Vayle's long-case clock in the Parlour has read 11:47
  since 1897, which on its dial is the same time). The seventh, in the Terminus, runs. Four of the six matter: winding them forward the missing
  thirteen minutes to midnight makes ZERO expect the run and open boarding; it logs no arrival (PaP steps).
- **The Three-Tone and the PA horns**: the Carrier made audible.
- **the 23:47**: clean, silver, unpainted, doors open, interior lights on. A bottle of champagne unbroken on
  the coupler.
- **The Dead-Man's Handle**: the brass master controller key of the cab. Without it the train cannot "depart".
- **The mercury-arc rectifiers** (Converter Hall): glass bulbs the size of a man with a pool of mercury and a
  dancing violet arc. They are the power switch and the map's most beautiful light.
- **The lever frame** (Signal Cabin): forty painted steel levers; eight still move.
- **ZERO's core** (Lodestar Exchange): racks of telephone relays that chatter in waves, two tape drives, a
  line printer that prints the timetable of the dead on fan-fold paper that has filled the room.
- **The Capsule Post**: Lodestar reused the Atmospheric's small-bore dispatch tubes to move parts between worksites,
  and bored them out to carry a rider capsule for inspection crews. The fast-travel system (`03-TRANSPORT.md`).
- **The Bellows and Vayle's organ**: a two-storey rotary blower with an organ console bolted to its casing.
- **Capsule No. 12**: Vayle's own passenger capsule, on its cradle in the Parlour's arch. Eleven were sent on
  the last day. His is the twelfth, and the only one with a window on the inside.
- **Token**: brass, the HRT disc cut through the centre. The **Zero token** has no bar: a plain hole.
- **Candy perk machines**: the film's product placement, as in every Wyler picture.
- **Strand's tape diaries**: eight micro-cassettes. **Vayle's notebook pages**: six. **Zero King murals**: five.

## 11. Secrets a player can find (in the map at launch, no main quest needed)

1. Strand's eight tape diaries (audio later; text in the Tab screen now). Order tells the truth of section 2.
2. Vayle's six notebook pages, each where his work was found.
3. The five Zero King murals; standing in front of each for three seconds logs it. Painted by Undercoat in 1985, together they are a map of
   the Capsule Post tubes drawn from memory: the painters had ridden them before Lodestar did.
4. The zero stud on Platform A: knifing it makes every clock in the station tick once.
5. Capsule No. 12 in the Parlour: its lid cannot be opened, but its window is on the inside and something has written the players' passenger numbers on the glass.
6. The line printer in the Exchange prints the current players' names on the passenger manifest.
7. The photo booth in the Arcade: paying 500 produces a strip of four frames; the fourth shows someone behind you.
8. The Announcer's Booth window (locked, "ON AIR" lit): a chair, a microphone, a cup of coffee that steams.
9. Three pay phones, three different events: the Fare Hall's rings once a match (a line assembled from the
   Voice); the Police Post's rings when the Night Inspector arrives; the Car Barn's rings only when lifted.
10. The hidden song: three brass skate-wheel trophies from the Orbit party, hidden in three zones; activating
    all three plays the map's own song. Decision: the earlier plan (a toy bear and a song from another
    publisher's game) is dropped for this map under the originality rule; the song is commissioned or generated
    for us (`11-AUDIO-DIRECTION.md`).
