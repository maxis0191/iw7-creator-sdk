# 13 · RISKS

What could make Station Zero fail, the mitigation, and the early test that would show it. Ordered by how much
of the project each one can sink. "Early test" means something that can run in the first two waves of
`12-BUILD-PLAN.md`, before the expensive work it protects.

| # | Risk | Why it is real (evidence) | Mitigation | Early test that reveals it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Visual quality stays near 5 of 10 however many rooms are built** | three rounds of builds and about 700 frames moved the old map by half a point; reviewers never said "ship"; the generator emits boxes with stock materials and no custom normal maps (`research/D`, `research/C`) | quality comes from the shipped maps' own props and room recipes (TF3, TF7), from designed two-state lighting, and from a reason for every object; the look is proven on two rooms (W2) before nineteen are dressed; a zone that cannot reach 7 is redesigned, not patched | W2: three independent reviewers on the Fare Hall and the Parlour against shipped rooms. Under 7 of 10 stops wave 3 and sends the work back to the tools |
| 2 | **No visibility culling: a map this size with 78 zombies does not hold frame rate** | everything is always drawn on a generated map; the shipped map has 17,010 props but hides most of them at any moment (`research/A` a) | budgets set from measures, not from the shipped totals (6,000 props first); props as compiled static models; small zones; frame rate re-measured after every dressed zone; TF5 as the planned answer; the stretch budget is locked until a culling result exists | W1's baseline, then a synthetic stress on the blockout: 6,000 stock props scattered by script-free placement plus 78 scripted zombies, measured with the performance recorder, before any dressing is done |
| 3 | **"78" cannot be delivered as the owner imagines it** | the engine and the official client are built around 24 agents; the earlier native attempt ended with invisible zombies and a crash (`research/A` i) | the hybrid crowd (E1, E2) reaches every player if its probes pass; the native route is the client capability CF2; the difference is stated in `07-ENEMIES.md` and must be stated to the owner in plain words when E2 reports, with footage | E1: one crowd body beside one real zombie, same camera, judged by reviewers for "can you tell which is which", and its cost measured at 54 |
| 4 | **The animation path does not arrive** | no Blender-to-game animation exists; no animation of ours has ever played in the game (C1 running, C2 open) | nothing in waves 1 to 3 needs it: weapons ride donor sets, enemies ride stand-ins, the crowd uses stock clips. If C1 and C2 are not closed when wave 3 ends, the map ships with stand-ins and the actors become an update |
| 5 | **Build time and the single build slot throttle everything** | 20 minutes a build for 9 zones; builds are exclusive on the machine; the old threads repeated builds hundreds of times | file checks first (2 s); TF1 so tests need no client; TF4 in wave 0, proven on the present map before the blockout; one build answers many questions; dressing packages queue | TF4's own proof in wave 0; then the measured build of the 19-zone blockout |

| 7 | **Zombies fail on level changes again** | the old map's wells: 0 of 10 climbs; a stair passed in one build and failed in the next | the stock stair construction on every level change, with the navigation-against-surface check; `sz2_navconn` before any build; the harness's climb phase at 90 % per case on every level change in W1; Rollers do not use stairs at all | TF2's offline check, then W1's climb phase: eight heights, every ramp, before any dressing |
| 8 | **Originality slips** | the earlier work leaned on a real agency name, two film nods and another publisher's song and toy | the canon list in `01-LORE.md` 3; the corrections in sections 7 and 8; the bear and song are erased; every art and audio brief carries a "must not look or sound like" clause; a name check is part of each package's review | grep of every text asset and sign against a list of real brands, agencies, films and games (W5's lint) |
| 9 | **The map does not install itself for players** | delivery of a custom map by a dedicated server is unproven; a failed download closes the official client; the map will be several hundred MB (the old build was 507 MB) | TF6 early; size budget per zone tracked from W2; textures at the smallest size reviewers accept | TF6 with the W1 blockout, not with the finished map |

| 11 | **Wonder weapons or perks turn out broken in real play** | numbers here are designed, not played; 78 zombies change what "strong" means | every number is in one table per weapon and perk and is a script constant; plans check them at rounds 10, 25, 40; each weapon has a non-damage brake; the owner's play decides last | the round-40 plans of WW1 and P1 in wave 2, with the cap forced to 78 by dvar on scripted zombies |

## The three tests to run first, in this order

1. **The stress number** (risks 2, 26; package ST1, right after the blockout): the empty big map + 6,000
   props + 78 bodies (E1's, or dummy animated models if E1 is not ready), at 1920 x 1080: frames per second.
   It decides the budgets of everything else.
2. **The two rooms** (risk 1): Fare Hall and Parlour to final, reviewed. It decides whether the method
   can reach studio level at all.
3. **The one body** (risks 3, 22; package E1, in wave 1, on a shipped map from a bench if the blockout is
   not ready): can it be drawn, animated and shot. It decides how "78" is delivered.

If all three pass, the rest of the plan is work. If one fails, it is known in the second wave and the
bible's fallbacks apply: smaller budgets, fewer zones first, stand-in enemies, a lower cap.
