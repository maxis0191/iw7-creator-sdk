# The Zero Kings

The **Zero Kings** were the paint shop of Halcyon Rapid Transit: the crew who kept the authority's cars in its green and cream, sacked in the cuts of 1979 when the Rapid decided that a train does not need to be repainted to run. They did not leave. From the winter of 1980 they lived in the dead-end siding at the west end of Platform A, which they called the **Boneyard**, and went on painting, first the authority's colours and then their own. There were about thirty of them. Their mark is a crown, stencilled on the back of the overalls.

## Origins

The name comes from the brass stud in the platform floor, the point from which the whole network is measured. The paint shop's chargehand, **Lionel Teller**, stencilled a crown on the shop door the day the notices went up, and declared that the siding at chainage zero was a kingdom nobody had thought to evict them from. It had no night staff and no lamp. The Rapid, which could not afford to evict anyone, kept the arrangement quiet. The crew settled in two wrecked car shells stacked aslant, set up spray booths of tarpaulin, kept their drums of HRT green and cream, and pinned their colour charts to the wall.

Their gear never changed: paint-crusted shop overalls, a respirator pushed up on the forehead, a crown on the back.

## The seal

In February 1982, a month before Lodestar won its contract, the Kings found the bricked arch at the end of the siding broken. Its bricks lay on the siding side of the wall, pushed outward: broken from the inside. They widened the hole and kept it to themselves; the Rapid never learned of it. Behind it was the Atmospheric's salon, and a brass capsule on a cradle in its arch. The Kings found the Bellows Vault as well, and rode the old dispatch tubes before Lodestar ever opened them out. They painted in the Vault, and nowhere in the salon, for reasons the walls do not give.

## December 1984

The Kings stopped painting in December 1984. Nothing in the Boneyard says why.

## Undercoat

One of them stayed. He called himself **Undercoat**, and lived through 1985 in the siding while the cleanup crews worked overhead. He found out what no one else had: that the dead obey the railway's colour code, which the paint shop had known by heart and the managers had forgotten. A dead commuter painted the right colour does what the colour says. He built a paint rig that could say it, and painted what he had learned on the walls as warnings. All five murals are his, painted in 1985. Whether Undercoat is alive is not recorded.

## The murals

Five murals survive, each six metres long, in spray paint, the only saturated colour in the station:

1. **Platform A West, on the pit wall.** A crowned figure holds the platform edge from below, and a white spray line begins at its fingers and runs off up the wall.
2. **The Sump, inside the culvert mouth.** A capsule drawn as a fat pill with a face at its window, the white line passing through it. Below, a tide mark painted as a joke.
3. **The Bellows Vault.** A mouth made of organ pipes, with the words "WE HEARD IT FIRST".
4. **The Boneyard, first.** A silver train with a face in every window and a crowned figure standing at the buffer stop with its back turned. The line ends at its doors.
5. **The Boneyard, second.** Four figures, one in each of four colours, walking toward the train. It is dated 1985 in the corner, though the heroes' colours were not yet on anyone's coat. Undercoat signed the other four with a crown; this one he did not sign.

Taken together the white lines form a map of the narrow tubes that Lodestar had opened out into the [Capsule Post](the-capsule-post.md). Undercoat drew it from memory: the Kings had ridden the tubes before Lodestar did.

## After

The Boneyard stands as the Kings left it: mattresses, a barrel fire, a foreman's chair made from a driver's seat, shopping trolleys of spray cans, a string of stolen lamps. Out of the shop's stores came a fare box rebuilt into a carbine, and the rig that taught the dead the meaning of colour. See [the wonder weapons](the-wonder-weapons.md). The Kings who were in the siding when the chime played at 23:47 were out of earshot of the public address and are not among the dead.

## In the map

The Boneyard is a zone of the Underneath district with the Kings' camp and stash. The five murals, all painted by Undercoat in 1985, are in Platform A West, the Sump, the Bellows Vault and the Boneyard (two). Standing in front of each for three seconds logs it. The Zero King is one of the eight kinds of the dead.
