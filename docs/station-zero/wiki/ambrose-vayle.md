# Ambrose Vayle

**Ambrose Vayle** (3 March 1851 to 1897) was an engineer, an organ builder's son and a spiritualist, who built the Halcyon Atmospheric Dispatch Company as a public convenience and, in private, as an experiment. He was never found after 2 November 1897.

## Life

Born in Tanner's Row, Port Halcyon, the only son of Josiah Vayle, whose firm of organ builders supplied three churches and a concert hall. Ambrose grew up inside the casings of pipes. His father held that a room has a voice and that a builder's business is to learn what it wishes to say; the son came to believe the same of a tube. He trained as an engineer, joined a Tuesday circle of spiritualists in 1879, and kept the two vocations apart in public and together in his notebooks.

## The patent and the post

On 12 March 1889 he patented "a dispatch tube tuned to its load". With a syndicate of organ builders and church trustees, his father's old customers, he built a 290-metre trunk main from a booking salon under what became Halcyon Plaza to the harbour exchange, with a ring of narrow bores to the counting houses, and on 14 May 1893 the [Halcyon Atmospheric Dispatch Company](the-atmospheric.md) opened it to the public as a pneumatic parcel post. The cylinders were driven by the breath of his great blower, the Bellows, with an organ plumbed in to hold the pitch of the main. In 1897 he added, for one season, "passenger dispatch": twelve padded brass capsules, one rider each.

## The forks

Vayle trusted a tuning fork over a ticket. In the Parlour he hung 144 of them on one wall, each stamped with a passenger class and a pitch, and every passenger was "tuned" at the wall before dispatch: a fork was struck, the passenger listened, and only then was the capsule sealed. He kept a brass listening horn beside the ring of velvet seats so that the waiting could hear the tube itself. He called both "courtesies". His letters record that children never needed telling to be quiet at the horn.

## The note

The main was tuned, he wrote, to a low C that is felt and not heard, the **Departure Note**, and tuned "to send passengers where the dead go, and bring them back". The public understood a quick ride. Vayle understood a destination.

## The Silent Dispatch

On 2 November 1897 eleven capsules, due back at noon, were found in their cradles in the Parlour, each with its rider smiling and dead. The twelfth, Vayle's own, was there too, and empty. Vayle had been seen in the Bellows Vault the evening before and was not seen again. The inquest returned an open verdict. In December the company was wound up and the main was bricked.

## Remains

Six leaves of his [notebook](vayle-notebook.md) survive on public view. His portrait hangs in the Parlour; its eyes have been cut out. Who did it is not recorded. His own capsule, No. 12, stands on its cradle in the arch, with a window that faces inward. Nobody has explained who rode in it, or where it went.

## Character

Courteous, exact, and visibly delighted by machinery. Contemporaries called him charming. His surviving letters are full of apologies for delays, which he blamed on the tube.

## In the map

Vayle's portrait hangs in the Parlour, where players also find the dispatch desk, the fork wall and the listening horn, his long-case clock and his capsule, No. 12, in the west arch. His six notebook pages are in the Fare Hall, Shelter 7, the Breach, the Parlour (two) and the Bellows Vault, where his work was found. His hand pistol, the Dispatcher, is earned in the Parlour.
