# Port Halcyon

**Port Halcyon** is a fictional north-eastern harbour city, with a population of about 640,000 at the 1980 count, built around a deep natural harbour and a plaza whose underground station is older than most of its streets. It is the setting of the film STATION ZERO and of everything in this archive.

## Character

A working port that lost its working. Through the 1970s the shipping lines moved their business elsewhere, the municipal bonds were downgraded, and between 1979 and 1982 the city's fiscal crisis closed two hospitals, a fire station and most of the night shifts on Halcyon Rapid Transit. The police could not be paid on time; the transit police, a separate force funded by the Rapid, were paid later still. In 1984 the city council wanted one success, and the Zero Line was it.

Districts that appear in the record: **Calder Road**, an old residential district with the line's first stop; **Marlow Street**, the commercial street on which the Orbit Rink stood; **Eastbank**, a shipyard district; **Tidewater**, the fish market; and **Harbour Exchange**, the harbour end of the line, where the Atmospheric Dispatch Company's tube once ended. The weather is rain, sleet and the sodium orange of wet pavement.

## The press

Two outlets carry the story. The **Halcyon Evening Ledger**, the daily paper, ran the gas incident on its front page of 2 January 1985 under the headline "PLAZA GAS MAIN FAILS, AREA SEALED". **Channel 9 Night Desk** held the broadcast side, and a one-year-after piece from the barrier was commissioned for the evening of 31 December 1985.

## The official story

The city's version is the **Halcyon Plaza gas incident**: a gas main rupture beneath the plaza, a precautionary closure, a cleanup contract awarded to Lodestar Signal & Telephone. The entrances were plugged and a quarantine canopy was raised. The city has not revised the account.

Port Halcyon's name has an old joke attached: *halcyon*, the calm days of midwinter, a quiet before something. Local people say it with a straight face.

## In the map

Port Halcyon is never seen, only quoted. Its name is on the transit police patches, the Channel 9 van at the Cordon, the newspaper dated 31 December 1984 on the Platform A West kiosk, and the missing-person posters of Malone, Prescott and Hughes. The film's poster hangs on a hoarding in the Cordon.
