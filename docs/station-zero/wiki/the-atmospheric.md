# The Halcyon Atmospheric Dispatch Company

The **Halcyon Atmospheric Dispatch Company** ("the Atmospheric") ran a pneumatic parcel post in Port Halcyon from 14 May 1893 to 2 November 1897: a ring of dispatch tubes round the harbour district, fed by a trunk main under what is now Halcyon Plaza, so that a letter, a ledger or a small parcel could cross the district in under a minute. For one season, in 1897, it also sold "passenger dispatch", a rider in a padded capsule. It was a convenience for four years and a rumour for the next eighty-six.

## Construction

The founder, [Ambrose Vayle](ambrose-vayle.md), built the post first under the Vayle Pneumatic Syndicate (1890 to 1892), a group of organ builders, bell founders and church trustees who had been his father's customers and who were persuaded that a tube could be tuned like a nave. The Company was incorporated on 2 January 1893. The trunk main ran 290 metres from the booking salon under the plaza to the harbour exchange, and the narrow dispatch bores branched from it in a ring to the counting houses. The main was lined in white glazed brick, "that the customer may see the tube is clean". Parcels were taken at the salon's desk for twopence; a cylinder left for the harbour every few minutes of the working day.

## The Parlour

The Atmospheric's booking salon, **the Parlour**, was its terminal and its pride: herringbone parquet, green panelled dado, white glazed brick, a coffered ceiling and gas chandeliers, a ticket desk behind a brass grille, and a long-case clock by Vayle's own door. Two things in it were Vayle's own, and nobody else's post office had anything like them.

**The fork wall.** On the east wall hung a walnut frame holding 144 steel tuning forks, each stamped with a passenger class and a pitch. In the passenger season no one was dispatched untuned. A clerk found the passenger's class on the wall, struck the fork, and the passenger stood and listened to it until Vayle's assistant was satisfied that the note had been heard. Vayle's notebook gives the reason in one line: the tube would carry only what agreed with it.

**The listening horn.** Facing a ring of velvet seats stood a brass horn the size of a carriage door, bent into the wall. It was connected to the trunk main. Customers waiting for a capsule were invited to sit and hear the tube's own note before they went into it. The attendant reported that most of them fell silent.

At one side stood the **dispatch desk**, four hatches of carved walnut, which sent messages and small parcels down a web of narrow bores to counting houses around the plaza.

## The Bellows

The post's engine was **the Bellows**, a two-storey rotary blower of riveted iron with a brass maker's plate, housed in a vault beside the Parlour. An organ console was plumbed into its casing, with its pipes running up the walls, so that the breath that drove the tubes also held the note of the main: the **Departure Note**, a low C that is felt and not heard. The Bellows was the tallest room in Port Halcyon that its customers never saw.

## The capsules

For the passenger season Vayle fitted twelve brass capsules, each a copper cylinder with a glass canopy and a buttoned red leather pad, sized for one rider lying back, with a numeral cast on the lid. Eleven stood in the public cradles by the dispatch desk. The twelfth, **No. 12**, was Vayle's own: it stood on a cradle of its own in the west arch of the Parlour, and it was the only one with a window set on the inside, facing the rider.

## The Silent Dispatch

On 2 November 1897, the last day of the season, eleven capsules were sent to the harbour exchange in the morning and were due back at the Parlour at noon. The attendant, Thaddeus Quill, found all eleven in their cradles at noon, though no one had heard them arrive. Each held its rider, smiling and dead. The long-case clock he had wound that morning read 11:47. The twelfth capsule, Vayle's, came home too, and was empty. Vayle was not found. The inquest returned an open verdict, the receivers wound up the Company on 20 December 1897, and the trunk main was bricked at both ends.

## Afterwards

The bricked arch under Halcyon Plaza was recorded by the 1931 surveyors as "old sewer". In 1983 Lodestar's excavation broke into the Parlour. Lodestar's express bypass was never driven through the main: the trunk was a bore for a post, and a six-car train would not have fitted in it. The bypass was wound around the outside of it, a pipe beside an organ pipe, and the Carrier leaked into the tuned main through the wall. Lodestar reused the narrow dispatch bores, opened out, as the [Capsule Post](the-capsule-post.md).

## In the map

The Parlour and the Bellows Vault are zones in the Underneath district. The Parlour holds the dispatch desk (one of the four Capsule Post hatches), the fork wall and the listening horn, the long-case clock (one of the four quest clocks), Vayle's portrait, capsule No. 12 on its cradle in the west arch, and its own perk machine, Bon Voyages. A single fork of the wall rings by itself when a zombie walks past. The Vault holds the BLOW and DRAW lever, the Bellows trap and the organ. The Dispatcher, the Parlour's wonder weapon, is Atmospheric property.
