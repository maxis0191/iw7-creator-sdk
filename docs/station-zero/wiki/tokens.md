# Tokens

**Tokens** are the Rapid's fare coins: brass discs, a little thicker than a coin, struck by the authority's own mint-maker and sold at the booths of every station. One token buys one ride, and is dropped into the slot of a turnstile, where it falls with a satisfying ring into a locked box.

## The disc

The standard token carries the Rapid's disc: a round face with the black horizontal bar of the logo, the held breath, cut right through the centre as a slot, so that a token can be told from a coin by looking through it. The rim is milled. On the back, "HRT" and the year. A token weighs about four grams and, in the winter of 1984, cost sixty cents. The Rapid announced a rise of ten cents from 1 January 1985, "the cost of the future".

## The token economy

Tokens were the currency of the underpass as much as the turnstiles. The shops of the Arcade gave change in them. Heel & Key took them for key cuts. The jukebox in Needle Drop took them if it was out of coin. The Zero Kings robbed the booths for them and used them to buy things from Rapid staff, who accepted them out of professional courtesy. In the last year the token was more reliable than the dollar.

## The Zero token

One token was struck in a different die. It was ordered by the chairman's office in November 1984 as the ceremonial first fare of the Zero Line, to be dropped in the gate by Chairman Dellinger at the inauguration. It has the Rapid's disc, with a plain round hole through the middle where the bar should be.

It was never dropped. It lies in a glass display on the shelf of the token booth in the Fare Hall, on a velvet pad, with a card that reads "FIRST FARE · ZERO LINE". The hole is a clean, drilled circle, a little more than a centimetre across. A Lodestar engineer who inspected it for the Chairman's office noted that it was not made to be dropped in anything.

What fits in the hole has not been established.

## Tokens as evidence

A dead commuter's pocket often holds a token. Cleanup logs record tokens found in the hands of the dead, in the coat pockets of the Night Inspector's marked passengers, and, one winter evening, in a pile in front of a turnstile that no longer works.

## In the map

The Zero token is on display at the token booth in the Fare Hall; the map leaves its hole open for the later main quest. Tokens recur as the ammunition of EXACT CHANGE, a carbine that fires them at 30 points a shot, and as the fines taken by the Night Inspector, who drops a pile for each passenger he fined when he dies; only the passenger who was fined can pick it up. Brass tokens also drop from the upgraded EXACT CHANGE and are worth 25 points to whoever picks them up.
