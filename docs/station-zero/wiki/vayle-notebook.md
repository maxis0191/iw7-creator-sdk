# Vayle's Notebook

[Ambrose Vayle](ambrose-vayle.md) kept a cloth-bound notebook of 112 leaves from 1889 until the evening before the Silent Dispatch. After 1897 it passed to the receivers and then to nobody. Six leaves are on public view, each where his work was found: the surveyors of 1931 framed one, civil-defence planners of 1961 quoted another, Dr. Strand pinned a third, and three stayed where Vayle left them.

**Page 1 · 12 March 1889** (framed in the Fare Hall as "station history")
"The patent is sealed. A dispatch tube, tuned to its load as a pipe is tuned to its note: the tube shall not merely carry the cylinder, it shall consent to it. My father held that a room has a voice, and that the builder's business is to learn what it wishes to say. I mean to learn what a tube wishes to say, and then to ask it a favour."

**Page 2 · 3 October 1890** (quoted on a 1961 poster in Shelter 7)
"The clay beneath the plaza is of such density that a bell struck below is not heard above. I sank a trial shaft today and rang a handbell at the bottom; the men at the top saw it swing and heard nothing. Here is a ground that keeps a sound. I have not told the syndicate what else it keeps."

**Page 3 · 21 November 1890** (pinned in the Breach, beside the glazed brick)
"The trunk is to be lined in white glazed brick throughout, that the customer may see the tube is clean and, seeing it clean, believe it safe. I have never known a man doubt a clean wall. Sample laid today; the mason asked why it is so cold."

**Page 4 · 7 April 1892** (the Parlour)
"The dispatch desk is finished: four hatches of walnut, glazed, with brass pressure gauges. Tested with a note in my own hand. It reached the harbour before the clerk had finished sealing the cylinder. I record this plainly and shall not repeat it to the board."

**Page 5 · 14 May 1893** (the Parlour)
"Opened. The first cylinders reached the harbour before the clerk had sealed the receipt, and the paper inside each was warm. I took the warmth for the friction of the bore. I have drawn twelve capsules in red leather for the day a person will want to travel as fast as a letter; the twelfth is mine, and has a window on the inside. The clock by the door I wound myself. Let the attendant wind it every morning, and let no one be late."

**Page 6 · 1 November 1897** (the Bellows Vault)
"The main is tuned: to send passengers where the dead go, and bring them back. Tomorrow eleven capsules carry a full complement, and the twelfth is mine. I shall hold the Note until they return, and not an hour sooner. If I am not at the Bellows in the morning, wind the clock."

## In the map

The six pages are found where Vayle's work was found: the Fare Hall, Shelter 7, the Breach, the Parlour (two) and the Bellows Vault. They are lore pickups and need no quest.
