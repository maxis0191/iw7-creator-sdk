Use case: product-mockup
Asset type: concept design image for a film prop, shown to the studio lead to choose a direction
Primary request: a clean three-quarter product photograph of one hand-held device. The object is a transit ticket-issuing machine rebuilt into a hand-held prop device for a film. It must clearly show ALL of these working parts: (1) a hand grip with a finger lever; (2) an issuing lever, a sprung metal lever that snaps down to stamp and eject a ticket; (3) a ticket slot at the front at the front end, a flat horizontal slot with the edge of one buff-coloured card ticket sticking out; (4) a ticket stack: a spring-loaded column of small stiff buff card tickets (each about 57 x 30 mm) seen through a window, with a follower plate; (5) a hinged stock door over that column; (6) a guard's dome bell, about 60 mm across, with an external striker hammer; (7) a row of exactly six small round amber indicator lamps; (8) a carrying strap. It has no barrel and no optics: it only issues tickets.
Subject: Direction D, "the dating press": a short heavy hand tool, about the size of a large stapler with a handle, made from a 1931 cast-iron ticket-dating press that the booking office kept in service and repaired in the 1980s: black crackle-finish cast iron body with a nickel-plated top lever (the press handle) that slams down, a vertical brass spring tube of card tickets standing in front of the grip with a slot window, a small brass stock door at its top, a polished brass counter bell with its striker mounted at the rear of the frame, and a brass strip with six small jewelled amber pilot lights (count them: six) along the top. A 1980s grey moulded plastic grip replaces the original, and one embossed label-maker strip reads "No. 0". A short wrist strap of black leather. A brass plate reads "HRT".
Style/medium: photorealistic studio product photograph, as in a 1986 industrial design annual. It is designed and built as a real manufactured product by a mid-1980s industrial designer working for a city transit authority: honest mechanical construction, visible fasteners, parting lines, engraved or screen-printed legends, wear from years of daily use. Believable and buildable. Era 1986.
Scene/backdrop: seamless neutral light grey studio sweep, nothing else in frame
Composition/framing: 16:9 landscape; the whole object in frame with a margin, front three-quarter view from the left-front and slightly above, ticket slot pointing to the left of frame, resting on the grey surface with a soft contact shadow
Lighting/mood: large softbox key from upper left, gentle fill, crisp speculars on the metal, neutral and factual
Color palette: black crackle cast iron, nickel plate, polished and tarnished brass, grey plastic, black leather
Materials/textures: real material response, fine surface texture, edge wear, fingerprints on polished parts, slight grime in recesses
Constraints: exactly one object; every part listed must be visible and readable; exactly six amber lamps; one dome bell; the card tickets are plain buff card
Avoid: fantasy ornament, runes, skulls, glowing energy, sci-fi panels, neon, holograms, steampunk gears and pipes, any real brand name or logo, modern tactical rails, text other than what is specified, watermark.
