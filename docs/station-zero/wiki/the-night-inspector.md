# The Night Inspector (Horace Pell)

**Chief Fare Inspector Horace Pell** (born 1933) served Halcyon Rapid Transit for thirty-one years. He joined in 1953 as a ticket collector at Platform A, rose through the inspectorate, and on New Year's Eve 1984 was chosen to check the first ticket of the automated era. ZERO still issues him a duty roster every night. Staff and cleaners know him as **the Night Inspector**.

## Career

Pell was the sort of public servant a fiscal crisis does not remove: indispensable, underpaid, and entirely unembarrassed by either. He recovered an estimated four million dollars of evaded fares over his career and wrote each recovery in a ledger, in pencil, in a hand that Rapid clerks used to practise. He believed that a ticket is a promise and an unpunched ticket an unkept one. His retirement was set for the spring of 1985.

## The roster

Every night at 23:47 the printer in the Lodestar Exchange prints a line that reads, in ZERO's fixed capitals, "PELL H · CFI · DUTY 23:47 · ALL CARS · TICKETS". It has done so since the Midnight Service. The Inspector keeps the roster.

## Appearance

He is short and very wide. The man who joined in 1953 was lean; the figure on the roster is about as broad as he is tall, in a brass-buttoned HRT tunic that no longer closes, on short bowed legs, with arms that reach his knees. Where the head should be there is a **hand signal lamp**, amber, with a peaked cap sitting on top of it and the cap's chin-strap buckled round the lamp's cage. The lamp is his face and the only light on him. His right arm ends in a hydraulic ticket punch the size of a rail tong. Over his left shoulder hangs a satchel, and from the satchel a paper ribbon of punched tickets trails behind him on the floor. In the dark he reads as a lit lamp at chest height above a wide black block, dragging a white tail.

## Method

He does not hunt. He inspects one passenger at a time, the one who has earned the most over the last two rounds, and fines them. Money a passenger has been saving counts for nothing with him; he reads what has come in, not what is in the pocket. He hurries. His walk is a brisk rolling waddle, and every ten seconds or so he breaks into a short scuttle with the punch held out in front of him. A marked passenger hears only, "Ticket, please."

He never uses a door. Brass inspection plates are set in the floor of every part of the station, each stamped "HRT INSPECTION". When he is called to a place the plate there rattles, lifts, and he hauls himself out of it. When the passenger he is following has left the zone, he goes down the plate he came up and, six seconds after that passenger arrives in another zone, climbs out of the plate there. A marked passenger who rides the Capsule Post or visits the Terminus keeps the mark. He closes the narrow dispatch hatches that stand near him, since he regards unticketed travel by tube as a fare offence.

Passengers found without a valid ticket, the Voice has said once, will be charged a penalty fare.

## The ledger

The ledger carries names. Pell wrote every fine he ever issued into it in pencil. Whose list he works from now, ZERO's roster or someone else's, has not been established.

## Death

When the lamp breaks the cap falls, and the tunic settles empty. Every fine he has collected is left in a pile for the passenger it was taken from, and no one else can pick it up. He leaves a validated ticket for each passenger present.

## In the map

The Night Inspector is the mini boss, from round 10 (round 11 solo), then every four or five rounds, never on a Roller round. He marks the player who has earned the most points over the last two rounds, skipping anyone who is away, downed or in the Terminus, and fines a tenth of what they hold. The lamp is his weak point, and every wonder weapon has its own way to open it; no wonder-weapon hit on him is worth more than 2,000, and none gets the lamp bonus. He arrives and moves between zones by climbing out of the brass inspection plates, one in every zone but the Staff Stair and the Terminus. His first kill of a match drops his ledger, a lore fragment, and a free perk purchase for each living player. A pay phone in the Transit Police Post rings when he arrives.
