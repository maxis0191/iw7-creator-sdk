# Behind the Film

**STATION ZERO** (1986) was written and directed by Willard Wyler, whose cursed pictures turn up in cinemas nobody remembers paying for. It is a 94-minute "transit horror", shot in thirty-one days in the autumn of 1985 on a set built in a disused tram shed, with the whole of Halcyon Plaza reconstructed down to the token booths. It opened on 7 March 1986 and was withdrawn after eleven days. No cinema has a record of the print leaving.

## Tagline and poster

The tagline is **"Next stop: nowhere."** The poster is amber on black: the Zero Line roundel, a clock with both hands at 23:47, and four silhouettes at the foot of an escalator with their backs to the viewer. The title is set in the Rapid's transit grotesque, and the credit line reads "A Willard Wyler picture".

## The shoot

Wyler cast four of his regular company as four archetypes of the city: the Transit Cop, the Graffiti King, the Signal Engineer and the Night Desk Reporter. The picture's whole joke is that its monsters are late, not hungry: a silver train that does not leave, a machine that announces it will, and a crowd of commuters who just want to go home.
The score is the sound of a public address chime, three falling notes, repeated at lengthening intervals for 94 minutes. The sound editor quit twice.

## Fake product placement

As in every Wyler picture, the film carries its own advertising, all of it fictional:

- **The candies.** Ten brands of sweet in vending machines throughout the station: Up 'N Atoms, Tuff 'Nuff, Racin' Stripes, Bang Bangs, Quickies, Deadeye Dewdrops, Double Takes, Baker's Dozens, Bon Voyages and Sour Grudges. Each has a machine with its own jingle and its own pun.
- **Orbit Rink**, "Roll Into Tomorrow", whose ringed-planet logo appears on the Party Car and in lit advertising boxes in the Escalator Hall.
- **Lodestar Signal & Telephone**, whose advertising box reads "The Future Runs Itself". In the picture it does.

## Reception

The *Halcyon Evening Ledger* gave it one star and the line: "The only train on time is the one that never arrives." A student magazine called it "the best film about a timetable ever made". Box office for eleven days: 612 tickets.

## In the map

The film is the frame of the map: the poster hangs on a hoarding in the Cordon, the tagline is its first message, and the candy machines are the perk machines (Up 'N Atoms in the Cordon; Double Takes, Baker's Dozens, Bon Voyages and Sour Grudges are the four new ones). The advertising boxes line the Escalator Hall. The four heroes are the cast.
